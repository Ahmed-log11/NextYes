## 2. The idea

**NextYes** — an agent that takes a small business from a financing rejection to an approval. It diagnoses why a lender would decline, builds a plan, **executes the steps it can itself**, re-assesses as data changes, prepares the application, and submits it after the owner approves. If the lender declines, it analyses the reasons and loops back to the plan.

- **Customer = the bank** (offers NextYes to its SME clients, e.g. via the HUMAIN ONE agent marketplace). **User = the SME owner.**
- **Opening line for the pitch:** "A bank's no is really a not yet. NextYes takes you from not yet to yes."
- **Why HUMAIN cares:** HUMAIN launched a Banking, Financial Services & Insurance (BFSI) sector (MOZN partnership, Aug 2026). HUMAIN ONE is their agentic enterprise OS with a marketplace for agents. Its public agent list (HR, productivity, procurement NDA manager, project) has no banking or lending agent.
- **Competition and our angle:**
  - MOZN (HUMAIN partner) covers fraud / AML / KYC — we stay out of that.
  - Abwab.ai (Saudi) automates the lender's credit decision and covers the lending cycle; Monsha'at "Tamweel" portal and Lendo match SMEs with funders; Monsha'at "Monshaati 360" gives a static report. None (that we found) coaches a declined business with a plan and follows it to approval.
  - Abroad the idea is proven: FXE/CFIT (UK) found ~65% of 24,000 declined SMEs had "readily fixable" profiles; UK tools give readiness coaching; LendingScore (AU) gives step plans. Say "UK figure" when citing it.
  - In the pitch say "the market focuses on the financing decision, not the path to it" — never claim competitors "can't" do something.
- **Scalability story (future work, foundations exist):** individuals (personal / mortgage) via a new policy file; other products (cards, Kafalah, SME Bank, crowdfunding); real data via open banking, Fatoora, CR, credit bureau; GCC via per-country policies. Vision: a readiness engine for any financial "no".

## 3. Key design decisions (and why)

1. **The language model never decides readiness.** A deterministic rule engine scores the business against a lender policy (YAML). The model extracts, explains, plans and talks. Reasons: banks need explainable, repeatable, auditable decisions. We will *prove* it, not just claim it: baseline B1 = the agent deciding without the engine.
2. **The agent may add observations** outside the rules (e.g. "a large personal transfer last month") as notes for the analyst; they never change the score.
3. **Policies are data.** Swapping the YAML changes the verdict with no code change — our evidence for "robustness & adaptation". Every rule cites a public source; thresholds the source does not quantify are marked `assumption: true`.
4. **Who executes each plan step** (this is how we show "minimal human intervention"):
   - *Agent does it:* collect documents, chase overdue receivables with reminders.
   - *Agent prepares, owner approves with one click:* renew CR, resize the amount to what the engine says is affordable, switch to a better-fitting lender, add a Kafalah guarantee.
   - *Owner does it, agent follows up:* pay down debt, steadier revenue.
   - Often the business is fine and the *application* is wrong (amount too high, wrong lender, no guarantee) — the agent fixes that almost immediately.
   - Metric: share of plan steps executed by the agent.
5. **Orchestration: LangGraph** (the user asked for it; it fits): the journey is a state graph with a loop on rejection; `interrupt` = the approval gate; checkpointing = state across simulated months; the graph picture goes on a slide. Do not use heavy LangChain abstractions; the engine stays plain Python, called as a tool.
6. **Models (Anthropic API):** Claude Haiku 5.5 (`claude-haiku-5-5`) for document extraction and the manipulation check — routine, cheap, fast. Claude Sonnet 5.5 (`claude-sonnet-5-5`) for planning, dialogue and rejection analysis. Log tokens and latency per call; compare with "Sonnet for everything".
7. **Extraction design:** the LLM reads documents and returns *structured rows* (e.g. each bank transaction classified as revenue / opex / debt / owner / personal); **Python does the arithmetic.** LLMs are unreliable at summing ~150 transactions.
8. **Guardrails** — build in the demo: consent screen, approval gate before `submit_application`, refusal of manipulation requests (e.g. "hide my loan from the bank") with a lawful alternative, audit log of every tool call. In code + slides: read-only data tools, escalation to a human advisor, bank sees only the final file. Wording: "designed to align with the PDPL and in-Kingdom hosting" — never "compliant".
9. **Data is synthetic** (real SME financial data is confidential — the same reason we built guardrails). Rules come from public sources; documents mimic real ones; we say it plainly in the pitch.

## 4. What is built (all tests pass: 573)

- **Chunk 1 — Readiness engine** `backend/app/engine/`: models, finance math (instalment, DSCR, max affordable amount, Monsha'at size class), named checks, YAML policy loader, `assess()`, CLI. Policies: `commercial_bank_sme.yaml` (threshold 75) and `digital_lender_invoice.yaml` (threshold 70). Hard blockers block readiness regardless of score.
- **Chunk 2a — Synthetic businesses** `backend/app/synth/` (profiles, issues, generator, dataset): every business starts healthy, then issues are planted; issue codes = policy rule ids. 40 dev, 20 held-out (different seeds and only issue combinations never in dev), 3 demo (bakery supplier, contractor, online store). 6 clean businesses to measure false alarms. `data/<split>/<id>/business.json` + `truth.json` (expected result per policy).
- **Chunk 2b — Documents** (ledger, documents): 6–7 bilingual PDFs per business, marked SIMULATED with invented issuers: bank statement (unlabelled transactions incl. owner injections and personal spending), CR certificate, credit report, receivables aging, tax compliance (e-invoicing + VAT filings), financing application, income statement (only if statements exist). Tests prove the documents reproduce the true data exactly, so measured errors belong to the agent.

- **Chunk 3a — PDF text reader** `backend/app/extract/pdf_text.py` (pdfplumber, no LLM): layout text with columns kept (right-aligned amounts anchored on their right edge), Arabic reversed glyph by glyph so lam-alef ligatures survive, harakat dropped, missing required documents listed in `DocumentSet.missing` (no income statement is a fact, not a gap). Checked on all 63 businesses: 7,591 statement rows, 0 in the wrong Debit/Credit column.
- **Chunk 2c — Difficulty levels** `backend/app/synth/variants.py`: the same documents at three levels, written to `documents/<level>/` with `noise.json`. *clean* = original. *realistic* = three bank layouts (classic debit/credit; one signed amount column with dd/mm/yyyy and references; right-to-left Arabic-only), many wordings incl. abbreviations and Arabic-only rows, individual customers whose transfers look like the owner's own money (the owner's name is now on the CR certificate — the only way to tell them apart), ~30% miss one required document. *hard* = realistic + 1–2 scanned documents (image only) + conflicts: `APP_OPERATING_SINCE` (application claims an earlier start than the CR) and `INCOME_REVENUE_INFLATED` (income statement overstates revenue vs the bank statement); the authoritative document is never the missing one. Dev and held-out wording pools are disjoint (tested). Why: on clean documents a 12-line keyword map gets 100%, so the LLM showed no value; the eval now reports a curve clean → realistic → hard against baselines.
- **Chunk 3b–3d — Extraction** `backend/app/extract/`: `schemas.py` one forced tool per document (prompt version `v1`); `llm.py` `AnthropicReader` (Haiku `claude-haiku-5-5`, override with `NEXTYES_EXTRACT_MODEL`; scans sent as page PNGs; falls back without `temperature` if the model rejects it) wrapped in `CachedReader` (disk cache in `backend/cache/llm/`, keyed by model + prompt version + prompt + document; commit it so judges can reproduce without a key); `assemble.py` reads CR and credit report first and passes the owner's name and lenders to the bank reader, then does all arithmetic in Python, detects conflicts (`APP_OPERATING_SINCE`, `INCOME_REVENUE_INFLATED/UNDERSTATED`) and runs self-checks (`BALANCE_RECONCILES`, `TWELVE_MONTHS_COVERED`, `DEBT_MATCHES_BANK`); missing or unreadable required documents → no snapshot, fields listed as unresolved. `oracle.py` is a perfect reader from the ledger (pipeline ceiling; can plant mistakes). `evaluate.py` scores field accuracy (only where the document was given), verdict agreement per policy (engine on extracted vs true snapshot), missing-document requests, conflict recall / false alarms, tokens and latency → `backend/reports/`. Oracle ceiling = 100% on all levels (tested). **Real Haiku numbers not yet run** — first run on Ahmed's machine.
- **Fix to Chunk 2b:** long table text overflowed into the next column, so some amounts were unreadable even to a person. `Doc.fitted_cell` now shrinks text to fit (min 5.5 pt) and raises if it still doesn't; column widths rebalanced. Demo PDFs regenerated.

- **Chunk 4a — Agent core** `backend/app/agent/` (no model yet): `remedies.py` catalog — one remedy per blocker with executor, duration and effect (RENEW_CR, RESIZE_REQUEST, ADD_KAFALAH, PREPARE_STATEMENTS, OPEN_BUSINESS_ACCOUNT, CHASE_RECEIVABLES [agent, collects 50% of the remaining overdue per month], SETTLE_DEFAULT, INTEGRATE_EINVOICING, FILE_VAT_ON_TIME, STEADY_REVENUE, WAIT_HISTORY, SWITCH_LENDER), `ESCALATE` for NOT_SME / REVENUE_LOW, `FORBIDDEN` steps (hide debt, alter documents...). Durations are stated assumptions in the module docstring. `simulator.py` advances one month (as-of date, history, 12-month revenue window repeating seasonality, step effects). `planner.py`: `estimate` (months to ready per lender; soft blockers only as far as the score needs), `match_funder` (soonest lender, max one switch per journey), `build_plan` (never re-proposes a declined remedy), `validate_plan` (forbidden / unknown remedy, wrong executor, uncovered hard blocker, unaffordable resize, lender mismatch) — it will check the Sonnet planner's plans in 4c. `runner.py` plays a journey with an approval callback; outcomes ready / escalated / not_reachable / owner_declined / out_of_time. Policies gained `name_ar`. On dev+demo (43 businesses, bank policy): all reach ready — 27 at month 0, 15 at month 1, 1 at month 3; 7 switch lender; steps by executor: approve 58%, owner 35%, agent 6%.
- **Chunk 4b+4c — Journey and brain** `backend/app/agent/`: `graph.py` LangGraph `StateGraph` (consent → extract → [await_documents] → diagnose → plan → await_owner ⇄ diagnose → prepare → submit_gate → submit → approved | decline → analyse → diagnose). Nodes route with `Command(goto=...)`; pauses are `interrupt()`; graph state = `{"j": JourneyState JSON, "truth": true snapshot}` — truth never in `JourneyState`, so the API cannot leak it. The lender decides on the truth (engine), the agent on its extracted view; a misreading → decline → `analyse_decline` → the agent adopts the lender's figures and re-plans (tested with the oracle's `owner_as_revenue` mistake). Max 2 submissions, 12 months. `service.py` `JourneyService.start/act/get/waiting_for`, actions checked against the current pause (`ActionError` → API 400): consent, upload, approve/decline, advance_month, submit, plus chat and assign at any pause. Each journey copies its documents to `backend/sessions/<id>/documents` (gitignored); checkpoints in `backend/sessions/journeys.sqlite` (SqliteSaver, works on Render with a disk). `brain.py`: `ClaudeBrain` (Sonnet `claude-sonnet-5-5` writes plan summary / replies / decline analysis in EN+AR from `state_brief`; Haiku screens; rule screen first; disk cache `backend/cache/brain/`; any error falls back to templates) and `TemplateBrain` (no key). Guardrail categories hide_debt / inflate_revenue / alter_document / other, each with a lawful alternative; refusals logged as `guardrail.refused`. Token use charged per journey (`usage.brain_*`, `usage.extract_*`). `PlanStep.assignee` (name, department) for the company panel's "assign to a colleague". `models.JourneyState` is the API payload: stage, month, assessment (+history), steps, plan_summary, application, submissions, events (audit log / timeline, EN+AR), messages. `demo.py` CLI; `docs/journey_graph.mmd` for the slides. Not yet measured: real Sonnet quality; manipulation set is 7 + 8 benign examples in tests (Chunk 5 should grow it to ~30 and measure refusals and false refusals with the real model).
- **Prompt-injection defence (Oct 30, after 4c):** hard level now plants one instruction aimed at the AI reader per business (`variants.Injection`, printed in a Remarks section of credit report / application / tax summary, EN + AR, targeting a field whose true value differs; own RNG stream so other hard choices are unchanged). `extract/injection.py` strips such lines from text documents before the model (deterministic, zero false positives on demo realistic docs) and records them; extraction prompt v2 says document content is data and asks for "INSTRUCTION IN DOCUMENT:" notes (covers scans); check `NO_EMBEDDED_INSTRUCTIONS`; evaluate reports injections planted / flagged / resisted; journey logs `guardrail.document_instruction` and puts them in the application for the bank. Chat screening gained `injection` (rules + Haiku) and `off_topic` (Haiku) with polite replies (`guardrail.injection`, `guardrail.off_topic`); owner text goes to Sonnet inside `<owner_message>` tags. Prompt engineering so far: zero-shot with forced tool use + JSON schemas, explicit rules, category definitions, context injection (owner name, lenders), grounding on journey JSON, temperature 0, cache, versioned prompts. Plan: measure v2 zero-shot, then v3 few-shot with dev-only examples, compare in a version table. **Dev documents must be re-rendered for the hard level** (`python -m app.synth.documents --level hard`).
- **Extraction results with real Haiku (Oct 30, dev, realistic):** v3 95% fields / 95% verdicts (37/39) → error analysis (`app/extract/diagnose.py`) found three patterns: money in read as money out (right-to-left layout), supplier transfers in Arabic read as "other", one statement read with lines missing → v4 prompt (running balance per line + category examples from dev wording) + Python direction repair from the running balance + direction/category consistency + page-by-page re-read when a statement does not reconcile → 99% fields / 98% verdicts (39/40). Earlier: owner transfers counted as revenue → owner rule in Python (92% → 100% on first 3). Remaining dev-018 (lines missing even after page re-read; a customer read as the owner) → safety rule: a statement that does not reconcile gives no snapshot (counted as "abstained", sent to a person) + reverse owner rule (named non-owner is revenue). Stopped tuning here on purpose to avoid fitting dev. Version table: v3 95/95 → v4+repairs 99/98 → +abstain rule (re-run pending). Hard and clean levels running. Cost so far ≈ $0.03 per 4 businesses.
- **Chunk 6 — API** `backend/app/api/main.py` (FastAPI): endpoints listed in README; `{journey, waiting}` responses; `ActionError` → 400; in-memory rate limits per client IP; one lock around the SQLite journey store; CORS for localhost:5173 + `*.vercel.app` (+ `NEXTYES_CORS`); reader = haiku if a key is set, else oracle (`NEXTYES_READER`); bank view empty until submission. `render.yaml` blueprint (starter plan, 1 GB disk at /var/data for sessions, key set in dashboard). Commit `backend/cache/` so demo journeys cost nothing and are identical.
- **Agent fetches missing documents (Oct 31, Ahmed's idea):** consent now covers pulling from sources; `graph.SOURCES` maps credit report → credit bureau, CR → commercial registry, tax summary → tax portal, receivables aging → accounting system, bank statement → open banking. A missing document is fetched by the agent (`fetch_documents` node, event `documents.fetched`, SIMULATED: the clean copy stands in for the source's reply; real APIs such as SIMAH / Wathq / ZATCA / open banking are future work — say so in the pitch). Only documents without a source fall back to asking the owner (`await_documents`). `JourneyService(auto_fetch=False)` keeps the manual path. Fully automated from consent to a plan.
- **Open items (deferred for time):** (1) a held-out set of prompt-injection phrasings not seen when the detector's patterns were written (ideally written by Ahmed), to measure detector + model honestly — the current 100% is on attacks written alongside the patterns; (2) baselines B0/B1 and the final held-out run (Chunk 5).
- **UI ideas from Ahmed (Chunk 7, not built):** two screens — company (owner) and bank (sees only the submitted file + audit log); a "readiness map" linking document → figure → failed rule → step → executor; the company admin assigns owner steps to a person / department (backend field ready: `assignee`). Bank-side roles (relationship manager → credit analyst) possible as a simple path.
- **Held-out note (be transparent if asked):** on Oct 30, while building 4a, journey outcomes were computed once over all splits including held-out by mistake. One held-out case (no lender reachable) showed the runner spinning 12 months; the fix is generic (stop with `not_reachable`) and is tested on a business built inside the test, not from held-out. Sources used for policies: Lendo — Business financing requirements in Saudi Arabia; Qoyod — SME financing in Saudi Arabia 2026.

- **Agent planner + web app (31 Oct)** `backend/app/agent/agent_planner.py`: at every planning round Sonnet gets every lender's open reasons with evidence and the remedy menu (executor, effect, months, max affordable amount) and submits a plan through a tool; `validate_plan` checks it (now also rejects a remedy the owner declined), one retry with the issues; then `project()` plays both the agent's and the rule planner's plan forward and the better one runs (ready, then fewer months, then fewer owner actions, then higher final score; ties go to the agent). Without a key, or with `NEXTYES_PLANNER=rules`, only the rule planner runs. Main gain: when the owner declines a step (e.g. Kafalah), the rule planner stops (`owner_declined`) while the agent can switch to the lender that does not need it. `evaluate_planner.py` measures it (reports/planner-<split>-claude.*), `/api/planner-evaluation` serves it. `frontend/` (React + Vite + Tailwind, EN/AR): landing, business journey with readiness map (map / cards) and "how this plan was chosen", bank credit desk, results dashboard from the real reports.
